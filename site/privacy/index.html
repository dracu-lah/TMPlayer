Privacy

TMPlayer is an unofficial Telegram video player for Android phones, Android TV, Windows and Linux. It talks directly to Telegram from your device, using your own Telegram account. There is no TMPlayer account and no developer-operated backend.

Who runs TMPlayer

TMPlayer is free, open source software written and published by one developer, Nevil (dracu-lah on GitHub). For the little personal data this page describes, that developer is the controller. Contact: hello@tmplayer.org.

What stays on your device

Telegram's TDLib keeps your signed-in session, cached chat information, thumbnails and the parts of videos you have watched. TMPlayer keeps your settings, favourites, Continue watching positions, the videos you download, and an update package while it is being installed. None of it leaves the device except as this page describes.

  • Android phones and Android TV: everything, downloads included, is in TMPlayer's private app storage. Other apps cannot read it, and uninstalling TMPlayer removes all of it.
  • Windows: the session and settings are in %LOCALAPPDATA%\TMPlayer, the watch cache in %LOCALAPPDATA%\TMPlayer\Cache, and downloads in Downloads\TMPlayer.
  • Linux: the session is in ~/.local/share/TMPlayer, settings in ~/.config/TMPlayer, the watch cache in ~/.cache/TMPlayer, and downloads in ~/Downloads/TMPlayer (or your language's Downloads folder).
  • A storage location you chose (desktop): the cache, downloads and update packages move to TMPlayer inside that folder. The session and settings stay where they were.

On Windows and Linux, uninstalling TMPlayer leaves these folders in place. Anyone who controls the device, or a rooted device, may be able to read them, so treat the device as a signed-in Telegram device.

While a video plays, its title appears in your system's media controls (MediaSession on Android, MPRIS on Linux, the media overlay on Windows), so the lock screen, notification shade, taskbar or a paired device may show it.

Services TMPlayer contacts

  • Telegram handles sign-in, chat and channel lists, file metadata and media downloads, under Telegram's privacy policy. Where Telegram shows sponsored messages, TMPlayer reports to Telegram when one is seen and when it is clicked, as every Telegram app must.
  • tmplayer.org, hosted by Vercel, is asked for one small file, latest.json, that names the newest release, when the app starts and every six hours while it runs. GitHub is asked instead when the site cannot be reached. Like any web request, it reveals your IP address, and it names the TMPlayer version you run. It carries nothing about your account. Settings can turn it off. An update is downloaded from GitHub only after you choose to update.
  • Your device's speech-recognition provider (Android TV only) receives audio only when you deliberately start voice search.
  • Sentry receives a crash report, and only if you have switched crash reporting on in Settings. See below.
  • OpenSubtitles.com is asked only when you choose Search online in the subtitle picker. A search sends the video's file name (or the parsed title, season and episode), its OpenSubtitles hash (a number made from its size and its first and last 64 KB, never the file itself) and the languages wanted. Signing in under Settings, Online subtitles sends your OpenSubtitles username and password to OpenSubtitles once; the password is not kept, only the session OpenSubtitles returns, and downloads count against your own OpenSubtitles account. Their privacy policy applies. Searches and subtitles are cached on your device for at most six months, and Settings can clear them.
  • SubDL is asked the same kind of search, with your own SubDL key, only if you have entered one under Settings, Online subtitles.

TMPlayer has no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK and no cross-app tracking. The developer never receives your Telegram credentials, chat list, filenames, viewing history or media.

Crash reporting, if you turn it on

Off on a fresh install, and off again after signing out. With the switch off, no crash reporting library is started and no server is contacted. It exists on Android phones and Android TV only.

With it on, a crash sends exactly this to Sentry, which stores it on the developer's behalf in its EU region (Frankfurt, Germany):

  • the exception and its stack trace, with file paths and anything that looks like a video file name replaced before sending;
  • the TMPlayer version and build number;
  • the operating system name and version (for example Android 14);
  • the device manufacturer and model (for example Xiaomi 23021RAA2Y).

Everything else the Sentry library would normally attach is removed on the device before the report is sent: device name and identifier, installation identifier, timezone, language, battery, memory, storage, screen, network type, boot time, app permissions, tags and breadcrumbs. No chat names, search terms or viewing history are included, and the IP address is not stored as personal data.

Legal basis: your consent, given by switching it on. Withdrawing it: switch it off in Settings and reporting stops at once; a crash that happens after that is dropped even if it was already being prepared. Retention: Sentry deletes each report automatically after at most 90 days. To have reports you sent deleted sooner, email the address above with the approximate date and device model.

Delete your data

  • Sign out (Settings) logs out of Telegram on this device and clears TDLib's database, the watch cache, your settings (the theme stays), favourites, Continue watching and the watched list. Your downloads are kept unless you tick Also delete my downloads.
  • Settings also deletes individual downloads, clears Continue watching and clears favourites.
  • Android: uninstalling removes everything TMPlayer stored.
  • Windows and Linux: after uninstalling, delete the folders listed above, and the TMPlayer folder in your Downloads or chosen storage location.
  • Your Telegram account is Telegram's, not TMPlayer's. To delete it, use telegram.org/deactivate.

This website

The website, hosted by Vercel, sets no cookies. It counts page views with Vercel Web Analytics, served from this domain as a first-party proxy to Vercel Web Analytics and processed by Vercel, which stores no cookie and no identifier that follows you between visits, and records only the page, the referrer, the campaign tag in the link (the support page link carries one, such as utm_source=about, so the app's links can be told apart; it names a button, never you) and coarse device and country information. There is no advertising script, no tag manager and nothing that can recognise you on another website.

The pages that show release information ask the public GitHub API for it when you open them, so GitHub sees that request. Hosting and network providers keep ordinary connection logs under their own policies.

Processors and your rights

The services that handle data for TMPlayer are Sentry (crash reports, only if you opt in), Vercel (the website and the update file) and GitHub (downloads, the update fallback and release information). Telegram acts under its own policy, not on the developer's behalf, and so do OpenSubtitles and SubDL when you use online subtitles.

Where data protection law such as the GDPR applies to you, you can ask for access to, correction of or deletion of personal data the developer holds, withdraw consent at any time, and complain to the data protection authority where you live. Since the developer holds no account data, in practice this means crash reports; write to hello@tmplayer.org.
